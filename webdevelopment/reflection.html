Reflection

My experiences throughout this program have reshaped how I think about teaching, learning, and the role of information in students’ lives. Each course has pushed me to consider how design, accessibility, and digital tools can support deeper engagement and more equitable access to resources. These ideas naturally align with the work of a library media specialist, where thoughtful curation and intentional technology use are essential.

Across my seventeen years in education, I’ve taught multiple content areas and grade levels, which has given me a broad understanding of student needs and learning styles. My experience in literacy, science and family and consumer sciences, in particular, has strengthened my ability to design hands-on, inquiry‑based learning experiences—skills that translate directly to makerspaces, research instruction, and collaborative library programming. Creating digital content, managing classroom resources, and supporting students as they develop real‑world skills all mirror the multifaceted responsibilities of a modern school library.

Pursuing an Educational Specialist degree in Learning Technologies and Design has further prepared me for this transition. The program has deepened my understanding of accessible design, digital literacy, and instructional technology integration—core components of today’s library media field. I’ve gained experience evaluating digital tools, creating inclusive online content, and analyzing trends in information access. These skills will allow me to support both students and teachers as they navigate an increasingly digital learning environment.

At the heart of my teaching philosophy is the belief that students thrive when they feel capable, curious, and empowered to explore. A school library is uniquely positioned to foster these qualities by providing a welcoming space for discovery, creativity, and connection. I’m excited to bring my passion for student-centered learning, my experience with diverse content areas, and my growing expertise in educational technology to the library media specialist role. I see this as an opportunity to create a vibrant, inclusive learning environment where all students can access the resources they need to succeed and develop a lifelong love of learning.

Shelly Linhardt - 2026
